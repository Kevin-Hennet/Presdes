At HackUMass XIV, my team and I built PresDes to make medication labels easier to read. Once we started working on it, we realized how much of the problem was just getting a good photo. The print is tiny, the label wraps around the bottle, and glare can wipe out a whole line.

With PresDes, you can take up to four photos of the same bottle, see what the AI was able to read, and listen to the directions and warnings.

I spent most of my time on the phone experience. I helped design and test the full-screen camera, the flow for taking and reviewing multiple photos, and the read-aloud controls. I also helped connect MongoDB Atlas so people could revisit and bookmark scans, deployed the app on Render, and set up GitHub Actions to run checks before releases.

My favorite moment in the demo was when we scanned a fictional label and PresDes pointed out that there was no dosing schedule visible. You could tap to see the original photo and check it yourself. That’s a small detail, but it’s the part I’m proudest of.

Next, we’d like to try comparing what PresDes reads with official drug-label references so we can flag anything that looks off.

Big thanks to my teammates and everyone who put on HackUMass. If you’ve ever had trouble reading a medication label, I’d love to hear what would’ve made it easier.
